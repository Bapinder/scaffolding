04

Generic trade job managers

What a lot of scaffolders actually run on. Tradify £34–£44 per user per month, Joblogic from £45 per licence per month, plus ServiceM8, Simpro, Commusoft, Fergus and BigChange. Quoting, scheduling, invoicing and payments are solid — but there is no scaffold vocabulary at all: no up/down state, no inspection clock, no handover certificate, no verification.

Read against our tracker

What they have, we don't
  • Quote → job → invoice as one chain. The main reason firms pay for any of these.
  • Variations and extra hire on the job — named explicitly by SMART and iRent, and the most under-billed thing in the trade.
  • Timesheets per job — without hours you see revenue, never margin.
  • Defect tracking on a failed inspection, with a rectify-by date.
  • E-signature on the handover certificate.
  • Customer portal and automatic document delivery.
  • Stock and dockets — multi-yard counts, delivery and return.
  • Offline mobile with geo-tag and timestamp.
  • Accounting integration — Xero, Sage, QuickBooks.
What none of them do
  • Two-person verification with the doer blocked from signing off their own work. Others record who did it; they don't require a second name.
  • Questions threaded on the job. Not present in any product reviewed — this lives on WhatsApp everywhere.
  • Hire money live on the card — days up × weekly rate, visible while it accrues.
  • No accounts. One link, pick your name. Everything above needs user provisioning first.
  • No licence cost. Five people on Tradify is about £2,000 a year; on Joblogic about £2,700.

Build order from here

Photos and QR tags parked for now. Ranked by money recovered per day of build.

  1. Variations & extra hire on a job — what changed, price, date, who approved it. Recovers real money for a day's work.
  2. Quote → job. Line items for erect price, weekly hire and adaptations; printable quote; "accepted" turns it into a booked job, completion produces an invoice summary.
  3. Timesheets on tasks — hours per person per job, against job value, giving margin per job.
  4. Inspection defects — fail an inspection, record the defect and a rectify-by date, which becomes its own task.
  5. Off-hire chase list — scaffolds standing past their planned dismantle, with a ready-to-send client message.
  6. Client pack — printable handover plus inspection history for the main contractor.
  7. Weekly print pack — what's up, what's due, what's overdue, one page for the Monday meeting.
  8. CSV export shaped for Xero or QuickBooks, instead of a full integration.
  9. Rough materials count per job — tube, board, fittings, and a yard total. The cheap 80% of inventory.
